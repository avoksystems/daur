# Avok Attest threat model, version 0.2

Licence: CC BY 4.0, Avok Systems Inc. Companion to the Avok Attest specification v0.2.

## 1. What is being protected

| Asset | Property |
| --- | --- |
| The deliverable bytes | Integrity: the buyer can tell that the file in hand is the file the record describes. |
| The record | Integrity and attribution: the declaration cannot be changed after signing. It is bound to the one organisation the signing certificate names, and to a person only when the certificate is a named-signer certificate whose common name is the record's accountable person; with an organisation seal certificate the person is the organisation's declaration, and verifiers say so. |
| The signing time | Fixedness: the time at which the declaration was made cannot be moved by the signer or by anyone else. |
| The revocation evidence | Fixedness: the revocation state the signer relied on cannot be swapped afterwards. |
| The verifier's trust state | Freshness: a verifier learns when its trust list is older than the one the signer expected. |
| The region hashes | Localisation: a reader learns which parts of a document changed, and which parts a tool touched. |

The record's truthfulness is out of scope: the mechanism makes a declaration attributable, time-fixed and tamper-evident. It does not establish that the producer told the truth, that the producer disclosed every tool, or that the deliverable is good.

## 2. Actors

- Producer: creates, signs and delivers; may later wish to deny or alter what was declared.
- Buyer or verifier: a contracting officer or auditor, offline, with the bundled trust list.
- Trust-list operator: Avok Systems for the pilot list; a buyer or consortium for its own list.
- Certification authority and time-stamping authority: the pilot CA in this repository, or an external PKI; for the pilot list, Avok's root and the public time-stamping authorities DigiCert, Sectigo and GlobalSign.
- Operator agent: for the pilot list, an AI agent runs the certificate authority commands and signs Avok's own records with an organisation seal, under the direction of Avok's owner (T19).
- Outsider: anyone who handles the deliverable and the envelope in transit or storage.

## 3. Threats and mitigations

| Id | Threat | Mitigation | Residual risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| T1 | Deliverable modified after signing (content, metadata, appended data). | `file_sha256` over the exact bytes; FAIL `HASH_MISMATCH`. Region hashes name the changed parts (`REGION_HASH_MISMATCH`). | None for byte changes. Semantic equivalence (re-saving a PDF) is reported as a change, which is intended. |
| T2 | Record edited after signing in the human-readable wrapper or in a C2PA copy. | The COSE payload is authoritative; wrapper and assertion copies are compared by JCS; FAIL `RECORD_ALTERED`. | A reader who looks only at the `.daur.json` text without running a verifier can be misled. The statement is always generated from the signed payload. |
| T3 | Record transplanted to another deliverable. | The hash binds the record to one file; the regions bind it to the file's content. | Since 0.2 an embedded C2PA carrier read without the source asset fails in the strict profile (`SOURCE_HASH_NOT_CHECKED`, with `C2PA_SIGNER_UNTRUSTED` and `C2PA_SIGNER_DIFFERS` when the claim signer is not trusted or not the DAUR signer). In the pilot profile it still passes with those warnings, and no trusted key is needed for that: anyone can re-embed the manifest in a changed carrier with any key, and only the hashed text regions are checked against the carrier. The exposure is to changes outside hashed regions (images, figures, pages or sections in no locus, metadata) and to records with no hashed regions, which could be moved onto another file. Buyers who accept C2PA carriers should require the source asset (`daur c2pa read --source`) and use the strict profile. |
| T4 | Signer back-dates or forward-dates `created`. | RFC 3161 token over the signature bytes; validity judged at `genTime`; `created` is advisory. Strict profile requires the token. The time is independent of the signer only when the token comes from another party (T20). | In the pilot profile without a token, `created` governs and the verifier says so (`VALIDITY_JUDGED_AT_CREATED`, and "Time taken from the signer's own clock"). |
| T5 | Signature made with a compromised or revoked key, or after the certificate expired. | Path validation at the signing time; CRL and OCSP evaluation at that time; `CERT_REVOKED_AT_SIGNING`, `CERT_EXPIRED_AT_SIGNING`. | Revocation evidence has latency. A compromise discovered later is addressed by T6 and T8. |
| T6 | Signer staples a favourable or stale revocation snapshot, or a handler swaps the snapshot after signing. | Protected header commits to the snapshot digests (label -70003); any swap or removal is `REVOCATION_SNAPSHOT_MISMATCH`. Revocation data must cover the signing time to establish "not revoked" (`REVOCATION_STALE` otherwise). | A signer can choose to attach no snapshot in the pilot profile (WARN only). Strict profile requires it. |
| T7 | Verifier runs with an outdated trust list (missing new invalidity dates or root changes). | Trust-list pin in the protected header (label -70004); the verifier compares identifier, version and digest and reports `STALE_TRUST_STATE`. | The signer chooses the pinned version; a signer who pins an old version does not trigger the warning. Operators publish the current version; buyers can require a minimum version in contract terms. |
| T8 | A certificate must be withdrawn from a chosen date because of compromise, debarment or misuse, and the CA's revocation data does not reflect it (or reflects it too late or too early). | `invalidity_dates` in the trust manifest; `CERT_INVALID_RETROACTIVELY` for signatures at or after `invalid_from`; earlier signatures stand. Applies to signer paths and TSA paths. | Depends on verifiers holding the new list version; T7 makes staleness visible. |
| T9 | Forged time-stamp token or token from an untrusted TSA. | CMS signature check, imprint check, critical `id-kp-timeStamping` EKU, TSA path to the trust list at `genTime`; `TIMESTAMP_INVALID`. | A TSA key compromise is addressed by revocation or by an invalidity date on the TSA certificate. |
| T10 | Root CA key compromise. | Trust-list manifest windows and versioning; a compromised root is removed in a new version and pinned envelopes signed afterwards will warn on old verifiers. | Envelopes signed before the compromise remain verifiable only while the root stays in the list; operators may keep a root with a `not_after` window to preserve older records. |
| T11 | Extractor drift: a different PDF text extractor reproduces different region hashes. | Extraction method identifiers pin the method family; mismatch on an intact file is WARN `REGION_EXTRACTION_DIFFERS`, not FAIL. Unknown methods are `REGION_NOT_RECOMPUTED`. | Region evidence is weaker across tool generations; byte ranges are exact and are always recomputable. |
| T12 | Label normalisation in C2PA libraries (`ca.avoksystems.daur.v1` reported as `ca.avoksystems.daur`). | Readers accept both spellings. | None. |
| T13 | A verifier hides warnings or shows PASS for a pilot-profile envelope without a time-stamp. | Specification requires warnings to be shown and codes listed; CLI prints "PASS with warnings". | A non-conforming verifier. Buyers should use the reference verifier or one that reproduces the vectors. |
| T14 | Browser verifier gaps. | The browser verifier validates RFC 3161 tokens in full and never emits `TIMESTAMP_NOT_VERIFIED`. Its gaps are: OCSP responses in a snapshot are not evaluated (in the strict profile the outcome is UNSUPPORTED, naming the capability and pointing to the command-line verifier; in the pilot profile `REVOCATION_MISSING`), and PDF page text and DOCX regions are not recomputed (informational `REGION_NOT_RECOMPUTED` with "the file is byte-identical to the one signed" when the file matched, WARN otherwise). It states each gap and never upgrades an unchecked item to a pass. | Users who need every check use the command-line verifier. |
| T15 | Personal data exposure in records. | Records name an accountable person and role by design; nothing else personal is required. Vectors use synthetic names. | Producers should name a role-holder, not private individuals without authority. |
| T16 | Misuse of the published test keys in `vectors/keys/`. | The root in `trust/` is the test root; its manifest says so (`ca.avoksystems.attest.test`, "Avok Attest test trust list (published key)"), and so do the README and `trust/README.md`. Since 0.2 every verifier treats it as a test list, by its manifest and by its published keys (an edited manifest does not change that): outside test mode the result is `FAIL (TEST_TRUST_LIST)` in both profiles, and in test mode every result is labelled TEST, the JSON result reads `TEST PASS` and a test pass exits with 4. A list that claims Avok's reserved identifiers without Avok's roots fails with `TRUST_LIST_ID_RESERVED`. | Before any real use, the trust list must be replaced by the operator's own list (Avok's is `ca.avoksystems.attest.pilot`), and `index.html` rebuilt from it (`docs/key-ceremony.md`). |
| T17 | Producer lies in the record (undisclosed tool, wrong extent, false review statement). | Out of scope for the mechanism. The record is attributable and time-fixed, so a false declaration is a documented, signed misstatement by a named organisation and person. | Enforcement is contractual. |
| T18 | Schema confusion: unknown fields or future versions smuggled past a verifier. | `additionalProperties: false` throughout; `daur_version` limited to the implemented values (`0.1`, `0.2`); `SCHEMA_INVALID`. | None within v0.2. |
| T19 | An operator agent that is compromised or misled (for example by instructions injected into content it reads, such as a deliverable, an email or a web page) while it holds certificate authority, time-stamping or signer keys: it could issue a certificate, revoke one, publish a changed trust list or sign a record that nobody accountable approved. | The agent signs Avok's records with an organisation seal certificate and never signs in a person's name, so a record never claims a person's approval the person did not give (`ACCOUNTABLE_PERSON_DECLARED` shows that the person is named by the organisation). No record leaves the organisation until the accountable person has read the deliverable. Certificates are issued only on the owner's instruction and recorded in the ceremony record; a wrongly issued or used certificate is revoked and, when the misuse predates the revocation, given an invalidity date (`CERT_INVALID_RETROACTIVELY`). Time-stamps for the pilot list come from public authorities, so the agent cannot set the signing time. Moving the root key to an offline machine operated by a person is the recommended hardening (`docs/key-ceremony.md`, section 8). | While the root key is online and the agent can run the CA, a successful compromise can issue a certificate under the pilot list or sign a record with the seal, and relying parties learn of it only when Avok revokes or declares an invalidity date. Detection depends on the owner reviewing the ceremony record and the issued-certificate index. Relying parties should weigh this until the root key is offline. |
| T20 | Time that is not independent of the signer: a time-stamping service run by the signer's own organisation can state any time the organisation chooses. | A time-stamp gives time independent of the signer only when it comes from another party. The pilot list lists only public time-stamping authorities for time-stamping and its own root for record signing only, so a token from a service Avok runs does not verify against it. Every verifier says who issued the time-stamp (`Time-stamp issued by <CN>, <O>`) or that the time came from the signer's own clock (11.8). | A list whose operator also runs the time-stamping service gives no independent time; a relying party sees the time-stamping authority's name and judges it. |

## 4. Assumptions

- SHA-256, ECDSA P-256, Ed25519 and the CMS and COSE constructions used are sound for the lifetime of the records (ten years for the pilot root).
- The verifier's software and its bundled trust list are obtained from a trustworthy source; the HTML verifier displays its trust-list version so that it can be checked against the published manifest.
- Private keys of producers are held with care proportional to the contracts they sign. The pilot CA in this repository is file-based; Avok's pilot root key is held on an online computer operated by an AI agent under the owner's direction (T19). Moving it offline is recommended before any third party relies on the list, and an operational deployment for third parties needs an offline or HSM-backed root.
- Buyers decide what a PASS means under their contract; the mechanism supplies evidence, not judgement.

## 5. Non-goals

- Detecting AI-generated text or images.
- Certifying producers, tools or deliverables.
- Judging the quality or accuracy of a deliverable.
- Conformance to C2PA; C2PA carriage is a transport convenience.
- Confidentiality: records are not encrypted; they are intended to be read by the buyer.
